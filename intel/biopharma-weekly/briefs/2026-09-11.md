# What moved biopharma this week — September 11, 2026

## Correction to last week's brief

Last week's brief missed the most consequential approval of its own publication
day. On September 4 the FDA granted accelerated approval to camizestrant, and
this brief was published at 5:04 PM ET the same day without it. The event is
written up as item 1 below and has been added to `catalysts.csv` with
`actual_date=2026-09-04`. The gap was a same-day timing failure, not a sourcing
failure — the item appears on the FDA's own
[novel approvals list for 2026](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026)
as Etcamah — and it argues for checking the CDER novel-approvals table as the
last step before publishing rather than the first.

## The three events that mattered

### 1. The FDA approved a cancer drug on the basis of a blood test, before any scan showed progression

On September 4 the FDA granted accelerated approval to **camizestrant** (brand
name **Etcamah**, AstraZeneca, NASDAQ/LSE: **AZN**) for adults with HR-positive,
HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer, **specifically upon
detection of an ESR1 mutation during aromatase-inhibitor plus CDK4/6-inhibitor
therapy**
([FDA novel drug approvals 2026](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026)).
The FDA's own listing frames it as an accelerated approval in the ESR1-mutated
setting, given with a CDK4/6 inhibitor
([FDA — What's New Related to Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/whats-new-related-drugs)).
Dosing is 75 mg once daily alongside abemaciclib, palbociclib or ribociclib, and
this is characterised as the **first US clearance of a cancer therapy triggered
by a resistance mutation found in blood, before imaging shows disease
progression**
([Life Science Daily News](https://lifesciencedaily.news/camizestrant-approval-ctdna-regulatory-endpoint/)).

**Drug class:** oral SERD (selective estrogen receptor degrader), used on a
ctDNA-defined switch.

**Why it matters beyond AstraZeneca.** The regulatory unit of analysis moved
from radiographic progression to a molecular signal. That is a precedent for the
entire liquid-biopsy layer — if a blood-detected resistance mutation can define
a labelled treatment-switch population, ctDNA assays move from prognostic
accessory to companion-diagnostic gatekeeper, with the reimbursement
consequences that follow. This is directly load-bearing for Lacuna's diagnostics
universe. It also arrives with an unresolved evidentiary tail: the same coverage
notes that as of September 8 there was no published NICE appraisal for
camizestrant in this setting, and the MHRA authorisation date could not be
established from the agency's published record
([Life Science Daily News](https://lifesciencedaily.news/camizestrant-approval-ctdna-regulatory-endpoint/)).
US precedent is not yet ex-US precedent.

One caution on the precedent's breadth: this is an **accelerated** approval in a
population defined by an on-treatment resistance mutation, which is a narrower
claim than "ctDNA is now a validated endpoint." The confirmatory obligation
still governs.

### 2. Myostatin finally cleared the FDA — and did it nineteen days early

On September 11 the FDA approved **Isembyld** (**apitegromab-mstn**, Scholar
Rock, NASDAQ: **SRRK**) for spinal muscular atrophy in adults and children aged
2 and older who are already on an SMN2-targeted treatment
([FDA novel drug approvals 2026](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026)).
The FDA billed it as the **first therapy to target muscle loss in SMA**
([FDA — What's New Related to Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/whats-new-related-drugs)),
and Scholar Rock describes it as the first and only muscle-targeted treatment
for the disease
([Scholar Rock investor release](https://investors.scholarrock.com/news-releases/news-release-details/scholar-rock-announces-fda-approval-isembyldtm-apitegromab-mstn)).
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/us-fda-approves-scholar-rocks-muscle-weakness-drug-2026-09-11/)
reported the approval the same day.

**Drug class:** anti-latent-myostatin monoclonal antibody — a class with a long
record of clinical disappointment.

**Why it matters.** Two things. First, it establishes an **add-on architecture**
in SMA: the label is explicitly for patients already receiving SMN2-targeted
therapy, so this is combination value layered on top of nusinersen/risdiplam
franchises rather than a substitution fight. Second, the class read-through is
the real prize. Myostatin inhibition is the leading candidate mechanism for
preserving lean mass during incretin-driven weight loss, and every myostatin or
activin-pathway asset in obesity now has a _regulatory_ proof point that the
mechanism can produce an approvable functional benefit in humans. That is a
different and stronger thing than a body-composition endpoint.

Note the date discipline: the PDUFA goal date carried on the watchlist was
**September 30**. FDA acted **19 days early**.

### 3. A CRL became an approval six weeks ahead of schedule — and the pattern is now a pattern

Also on September 11, the FDA approved **Joenja** (**leniolisib**, Pharming
Group, NASDAQ/Euronext: **PHAR**) as the first treatment for **children aged 4
to 11** with activated PI3K-delta syndrome (APDS) who weigh at least 27 kg
([Pharming](https://www.pharming.com/our-news/fda-approves-pharming-joenja-as-first-treatment-for-children-with-apds-in-the-us));
the FDA logged it the same day as the first treatment for that age group
([FDA — What's New Related to Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/whats-new-related-drugs)).
This application had previously **received a complete response letter**, and the
resubmitted sNDA was only accepted on June 4
([Pharming](https://www.pharming.com/our-news/pharming-announces-u-s-fda-acceptance-of-snda-resubmission-for-joenja)).
Its PDUFA date on the watchlist was **October 24** — FDA acted **43 days
early**.

Set against Isembyld's 19 days and Bayer's accelerated approval of
**sevabertinib** (**Hyrnuo**) on September 9 for first-line HER2-mutant
non-squamous NSCLC
([FDA oncology approval notification](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-sevabertinib-locally-advanced-or-metastatic-non-squamous-non-small);
[Bayer](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/us-fda-grants-accelerated-approval-to-bayers-targeted-therapy-sevabertinib-as-a-first-line-treatment-option-for-adults-with-her2-mutant-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/)),
three separate approvals landed inside one week, two of them well before their
goal dates.

**Why it matters operationally.** If FDA action dates are becoming ceilings
rather than targets, then a watchlist keyed to PDUFA dates systematically looks
in the wrong place. A calendar built on goal dates will mark rows resolved
_after_ the market has already moved. This is an argument for tracking
`actual_date` separately from `scheduled_date` — which the 19-column schema now
does — and for treating any priority-review row inside its final 60 days as live
rather than pending.

One honest limit: three early actions in one week is an anecdote, not a trend.
The measurable version of this claim is the median gap between `scheduled_date`
and `actual_date` across resolved rows, and the watchlist does not yet hold
enough resolved rows to compute it credibly.

## Also notable

- **A women's-health readout with no incumbent to displace.** On September 10,
  AbbVie (NYSE: **ABBV**) reported positive Phase 3 **LUNA** topline results for
  **atogepant** (QULIPTA in the US, AQUIPTA in the EU) in the **preventive
  treatment of menstrual migraine**, meeting the primary endpoint and all eight
  ranked secondary endpoints, in both pure menstrual migraine and
  menstrually-related migraine
  ([StockTitan release coverage](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/ABBV/abb-vie-extends-migraine-leadership-with-positive-phase-3-atogepant-1dm9btt4jtvf.html)).
  There is **no approved treatment** for this indication. AbbVie says it will
  submit to health authorities worldwide; **no PDUFA date exists yet**, so this
  is a readout row, not a decision row. Drug class: oral CGRP receptor
  antagonist. For a women's-health portfolio this is the week's most relevant
  item after camizestrant — a large, chronically under-treated, cyclically-timed
  indication being addressed by an already-approved molecule, which compresses
  both development risk and launch cost.
- **Telix's PDUFA date passed without a public decision.** The action date for
  **TLX101-Px** (Pixclara, floretyrosine F 18) in glioma imaging was **September
  11**
  ([Telix](https://telixpharma.com/news-views/fda-accepts-nda-for-tlx101-px-pixclara/)).
  No approval or CRL had been announced as of this brief. The row stays
  `upcoming` rather than being marked resolved — Telix has previously received a
  CRL on a related glioma imaging NDA, TLX101-CDx
  ([Telix regulatory update](https://ir.telixpharma.com/static-files/e40a4bf3-7398-4dbd-82eb-feb10e9295fc)),
  so silence should not be read either way.
- **A new advisory committee was scheduled, and this one is real.** The
  **Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee** will meet
  **October 1, 2026**, 9:00 a.m.–1:00 p.m. ET, on strain selection for influenza
  vaccines for the **2027 Southern Hemisphere** season
  ([Federal Register notice, published September 9, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/09/2026-18349.txt)).
  Flagging the provenance deliberately: last week's brief carried a fabricated
  advisory-committee row sourced to an undated aggregator page whose listings
  were from 2019–2020 and which referenced the _2020_ Southern Hemisphere
  influenza season. This row is sourced to a dated Federal Register document
  instead.
- **Two aggregator errors caught while building the October rows.** First,
  [pdufa.bio's October calendar](https://www.pdufa.bio/calendar/2026/october)
  lists "VTRS · MR-141" and "IRD · Phentolamine ophthalmic solution 0.75%" as
  two separate October 17 decisions. They are one application: Viatris and Opus
  Genetics announced the same presbyopia sNDA with the same October 17 goal date
  on the same day
  ([Viatris](https://newsroom.viatris.com/2026-02-25-FDA-Accepts-Viatris-Supplemental-New-Drug-Application-for-MR-141-Phentolamine-Ophthalmic-Solution-0-75-for-the-Treatment-of-Presbyopia);
  [Opus Genetics](https://ir.opusgtx.com/press-releases/detail/519/opus-genetics-announces-fda-acceptance-of-supplemental-new-drug-application-for-phentolamine-ophthalmic-solution-0-75-for-the-treatment-of-presbyopia)).
  One row was added, not two. Second,
  [TheraRadar](https://theraradar.com/dashboards/pdufa-calendar/) lists Inovio's
  October 30 decision under **cervical intraepithelial neoplasia**; INO-3107 is
  a DNA immunotherapy against HPV-6 and HPV-11 for **recurrent respiratory
  papillomatosis**
  ([Inovio Q2 2026 results](https://ir.inovio.com/news/news-details/2026/INOVIO-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-and-Recent-Business-Highlights/default.aspx)).
  Had that gone in unchecked it would have entered the watchlist as a
  women's-health row, which it is not.
- **Winrevair's September 21 date is real, but mis-typed on the watchlist.** The
  **HYPERION**-based label expansion for **Winrevair** (sotatercept-csrk, Merck,
  NYSE: **MRK**) to newly diagnosed, first-line PAH patients carries a September
  21 action date
  ([pdufa.bio September 2026 calendar](https://www.pdufa.bio/calendar/2026/september);
  [AppliedXL](https://www.appliedxl.com/news/winrevair-seeking-label-update-for-winrevair-pah-fda-decision-2026)).
  The watchlist described it as an sNDA; sotatercept is a biologic reviewed
  under BLA 761363
  ([FDA supplement approval letter](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/761363Orig1s010ltr.pdf)),
  so the row now reads sBLA.
